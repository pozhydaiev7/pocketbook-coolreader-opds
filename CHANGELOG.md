### Dictionary: a word of the lowest line, a word of the list; the menu of a bookmark (alpha2)

Three remarks of a tester, with `0.0.8.1-opds`.

- **A word of the lowest line of the page could not be chosen with the dictionary window open** (`LVDocView::getCursorRect`, `crengine/src/lvdocview.cpp`; `CRPbDictionaryDialog::onTouchEvent`, `_touchOnBook`, `cr3gui/src/cr3pocketbook.cpp`). The tester: with the window open, a tap on a word of the lowest line makes the window "jump" down and open an article or the word list "by a rule one cannot see"; with the window closed, the word of the lowest line is chosen by a long tap, and the window covers it. The window goes to the half of the screen away from the word, and it asks the view where the word is. The view gave no place for any word of the lowest line: the bottom of the rectangle of such a word is the first row of the next page, and it was asked for as a point of the page (`docToWindowPoint` has the argument for the bottom of a rectangle, it was not used here). A word without a place counted as a word at the top, so the window went to the bottom, over the word and under the finger; the lift of the finger then came to the window, as a tap on its article or on its list. The bottom is asked for as the bottom of a rectangle. A touch that began over the book also stays a touch of the book where the window comes under the finger. The links of the lowest line had the same fault in the list of the links of a page (`CRLinksDialog`, `cr3gui/src/linksdlg.cpp`): no place, so they could not be tapped there.
- **Checked on the PC** (`wordtest.cpp`, the engine of the clone in the bench tree `a18`): the place of every word of 40 pages asked for as the window asks, in an FB2 with the font sizes 30, 44 and 60 and the line spacings 80, 100 and 120 %, with and without the status line, a larger FB2, an EPUB and the HTML file of the tester. Before: no place for every word of a lowest line (227 to 446 words of a run), and the window sent over the word for all of them on a page filled below its middle. Now: 0 words without a place, 0 times the window is sent over the word.
- **A word of the word list opens its own article** (`CRPbDictionaryView::openListWord`, `CRPbDictionaryMenu::getCurItemTranslation`, `cr3gui/src/cr3pocketbook.cpp`). The tester: when a dictionary has a word with a prefix sign and the same word without it ("-appointed" and "appointed"), the word without the prefix stands second in the list and does not open from it. A tap on a row looked its word up again and showed what the lookup gave: the dictionary of the firmware gives "-appointed" for "appointed". The row of the word looked up last (the first row of a list opened from its article) did nothing at all: it was "the same word". The tap shows the article the row has. With "Selected dictionaries" the word is looked up in all of them first, and the article of the row is shown when that gives no article.
- **A tap anywhere on a row of the word list opens its article** (`CRPbDictionaryView::onTouchEvent`, `cr3gui/src/cr3pocketbook.cpp`). The right third of the list turned its page, the upper half back and the lower half forward, from the time the list had no swipe. The owner, trying the list: the article opens by a tap on the word in bold only, and a tap on the translation beside it leafs the list, "not intuitive at all". The whole row opens the article; the list is leafed by a vertical swipe and by the keys, as the manual tells.
- **The word itself is looked for beside the word the firmware gives** (new `cr3gui/src/pb_dicword.h`, `pbDicFindWord`; `PbInkviewDict::find`, `lookup`, `seek`, `cr3gui/src/pb_dict.cpp`). When the word the firmware gives differs from the word asked for by characters that are no letters and no digits, up to 8 words after it and 8 before it are looked through for the word itself, as far as they differ in that way only; the dictionary is left at the word returned. So a word of the book opens the article of "appointed", not of "-appointed", and the next page of the word list goes on from the word it ended with (from "-appointed" it began with "appointed" once more). A word found at once, or one the dictionary does not have, takes one lookup as before. PC test of the rule with a stand-in for the dictionary (`dicword_test.cpp`): 31 checks, none failed (both words, three words of the same letters, the lookup landing on the first of them or in their middle, the letter case, a word not in the dictionary). **What the firmware gives for such words is known from the report of the tester only**: it is not checked on a reader with his dictionary.
- **The menu of a bookmark closed without a choice** (`pbFirmwareMenuClosed`, `pbMenuTapInput`, `pbMenuRedrawTimer`, `cr3gui/src/cr3pocketbook.cpp`; the handlers of the menus in `bmkdlg.cpp`, `recentdlg.cpp`, `pb_pageview.cpp`). The tester: the menu of a long press in the list of the bookmarks cannot be left without choosing one of its rows, a tap beside it does not close it. The menu is one of the firmware (`OpenMenu`), and a tap beside it is taken by the firmware. What the firmware does with that tap cannot be seen on the PC, so three things in the code that fit the report are closed: (1) the list of the bookmarks of a PDF or a DjVu did not take the next tap after such a menu (it still waited for the lift of the long press, which the menu had taken); (2) the lists of the book bookmarks and of the recent books sent the "no choice" of the menu on as a command; (3) nothing guarded the list from the tap that closed the menu, should the firmware give it, or its lift, to the program as well: the list would set the bookmark or go to it. - **What the tester sent next**: the history file of his reader and its copy before the last write (`system/state/cr3/.cr3hist`, `.cr3hist.bak`), 33 records each, the most the program keeps. The copy has "Второе нашествие марсиан" first, kept at 80.79 %, and the record of `othworld.htm` second, 45 minutes old, at the first page; the file, written 44 seconds later, has `othworld.htm` first, at the first page again. A book read for days in one copy keeps its place, which is why his FB2 book did. It has nothing to do with HTML, and nothing with the pointer fixed in `0.0.8.1-opds`. Not seen directly: the older copy writes no log (the log is turned on in the newer one), so the write that drops the record is read off the two files, not off a log line.

A file that is not there or is not read is written from the memory of the copy, as before. The file is not written when it would get the bytes it has (it was compared with what this copy had written last; now with the file itself). The log has a line for every save (`history: the record of the open book ... goes first ...`) and one when another copy has written the file since this one read or wrote it (`history: another copy of the program has written ...`).

### One copy of the program at a time (alpha1)

- **The question of the owner**: can the program be kept to one copy. The history is one file of several that every copy writes whole: the settings are another, and the queue of the position sync lies in the settings. A copy of the program also holds its book in memory.
- **A start that finds a running copy gives it the book and ends** (new `cr3gui/src/pb_singlecopy.h`; `pbInstancePublish`, `pbInstanceWithdraw`, `pbHandBookToRunningCopy`, `pbTakeOpenRequest`, `pbOpenHandedBook`, `pbTaskRows`, `pbCopiesFileLog`, `cr3gui/src/cr3pocketbook.cpp`; `data/common/system/bin/cr3-pb.app`). The owner: let the program that starts see the copy that runs, ask it to open the other book and close itself; and: how does KOReader do it. KOReader does exactly that on these readers, in its launcher script (`platform/pocketbook/koreader.app`, `tryOpenBook` of `frontend/device/pocketbook/device.lua`): the path of the book is left in a file, the running instance is given the screen by the tool of the firmware (`iv2sh SetActiveTask`), the start ends, and the instance opens the book when it comes to the screen. The same here. The copy that runs names its task and its process in `/tmp/cr3-pb.instance` when it shows its first page, and takes the name back when it ends or closes its book. The launcher script, before it starts the program, reads that file, checks that the process named is the program (`/proc/<pid>/cmdline`), writes the book to `/tmp/cr3-pb.open` and runs `iv2sh SetActiveTask` in its own place. Where the script could not (no `iv2sh`), the program does the same before it loads anything (`SetActiveTask` of libinkview). The running copy, at `EVT_FOREGROUND`, takes the file and opens the book the way the first file is opened (a book, a PDF or a DjVu as a book or as its pages); what it was showing is kept first (the position, the history), and when the new file does not open, the one that was open is opened again. The task is then named by its new book for the firmware (`pbTellTaskBook`).
- **What it gives.** One process writes the settings, the history and the queue of the position sync. The second book needs no start of the program (fonts, settings, skin). No copy is ended by another.
- **The first run on the reader of the owner** (InkPad 4, firmware 6.11). The firmware has `/usr/bin/iv2sh`: the launcher gave the book away six times, and one copy (one process, one task) opened the six books, each 224 to 365 ms after it came to the screen, at the place it was left at; no other copy ran. The remark of the owner: a pause after the tap, then the book read before for a moment, then the new one. The pause is in the log: after every Home key the copy wrote its files and asked the library of the firmware for the page of the book (`bsLoad`), which gave nothing after 5.02 to 5.05 s, every time; a book tapped within those seconds was opened only after them (4.5 to 5 s later, five times of six).

The program is free at once after the Home key. `CRPocketBookGlobals::setFileName` forgets the page told last when the book changes, so that the page of a book given to the copy is told even when it has the number of the page of the book before. **The next run on that reader**: asked by the thread, the library answered every time, `bsLoad` in 36 to 197 ms and `bsSave` saved, where it had given nothing after 5 s to the program itself six times of six; a book tapped right after the Home key came to the copy 0.3 to 0.9 s after the launcher and was opened in 334 to 547 ms; the events of the firmware came as before, and the program ended the proper way. To have the new book there at once the copy would have to open it before it gets the screen, which needs a way to wake a copy in the background; not made.

## 0.0.8.1-opds — 2026-11-25

The release: the code of `0.0.8.1-opds-alpha2` with the version of the release, nothing else changed in the program; the README names the search over the pages of a PDF and a DjVu. Test builds: `0.0.8.1-opds-alpha1`, `0.0.8.1-opds-alpha2`.

### The clock of the status line did not run with the status line at the top (alpha2)

- **Seen on the reader of the owner**: the clock stood still for two minutes, and no log of the reader, of this build or of the ones before, has a line of the clock. The timer of the clock was never set: `pbClockWanted` (`cr3gui/src/cr3pocketbook.cpp`) took the value 0 of the setting of the status line for "off", and 0 is "at the top" in the PocketBook settings (1 at the bottom, 2 off), the setting of the owner and the only one the settings menu offers besides "off". The clock of alpha1 has therefore never run on a reader; the PC bench had tested the drawing, not this check.

### The position sync does not hold the program (alpha2)

- **The remark of the owner**, from a log of the reader: "ugly lags of Wi-Fi and of the sync of the book". The log: a book opened with the sync on and Wi-Fi off, the firmware turned Wi-Fi on in 23.6 s (`NetConnectSilent`), with an hourglass and the program not answering; then three requests of 0.4 to 0.8 s each (the places kept asked and sent, the place of the book asked).
- **The automatic sync is made by a thread** (`AutoJob`, `autoJobRun`, `PbKosync::autoPull`, `autoFinish`, `shutdown`, `takeSentOffQueue`, `pulled`, `cr3gui/src/pb_kosync.cpp`). A book opened, the program back on the screen and Wi-Fi turned on start a job with a copy of what it needs (the account, the places kept, the book); the thread turns Wi-Fi on, sends the places kept and asks for the place, and touches nothing of the program. A timer of the program takes what it brought back: the places sent leave the queue as it is then (a place may have been kept meanwhile), and the place of the server is weighed against the place the book is at then, if it is the same book still. No hourglass: the reader turns pages meanwhile. One job at a time; a request that comes meanwhile (another book opened while Wi-Fi was coming up) is made when the job ends (`_autoAgain`). The timer is a hard one: a weak one waits for the next event while the device sleeps between the events, and the place of the server would come at the next touch. When the program is closed the job is told to stop (`AutoJob::abandoned`, read by the requests through `kosync::Account::stop`: no request is started, in the retries of one and between the places of the queue); a thread that is in a request is waited for, it ends within the limits of that request (2 s and 5 s), and is gone before the engine is shut down; a thread that is in the firmware turning Wi-Fi on is not waited for (half a minute, not to be stopped): the job is left to it, and it touches nothing but the job when the firmware returns. Which of the two it is, is known for sure (`AutoJob::state`). The thread starts with the signals blocked, so that those of the program stay with the thread of the program; an event of the firmware that comes in another thread than that of the program is left out and told in the log (`pbMainThread`, `main_handler`). "Send the place" and "Get the place" wait for the thread while it turns Wi-Fi on, and do not ask the firmware for a second connection (`ensureNetwork`). After the program is closed no automatic request is started (`_closed`, the timers taken off); Wi-Fi the firmware has just failed to turn on is not asked for again at once by a request that waited.
- **A program started by this one writes no line of the log before it starts** (`pbLaunchWaitBinary`, `cr3gui/src/cr3pocketbook.cpp`; the front light, the task manager): the child of `fork()` wrote two lines, and a lock of the logger held by the thread at that moment stays taken in the child for ever; the child waited for it, the program for the child. A program that does not start ends the child with `_exit`.
- **One request at a time, whichever thread makes it** (`WebLock`, `cr3gui/src/web.cpp`): the handle kept between the requests, the login of a catalog and the TLS library of the firmware are shared. A request inside another one of the same thread (the progress callback of a download) goes on as before. **The logger has a lock** (`CRLogLock`, `crengine/src/crlog.cpp`): the functions that write a line and the ones that replace or delete the logger take it, so that the thread does not write through a logger the program has just freed (the log turned on or off in "Debugging", the engine shut down), and a line is written whole; the time of a line is taken with `localtime_r`.
- **Left as it is**: the place sent when the book is closed or the program leaves the screen (one request, about half a second, behind the home screen), the actions "Send the place" and "Get the place" (asked for by the reader, with an hourglass), and the 5 seconds the book state of the firmware takes when the program leaves the screen (`bsLoad gave nothing in 5040 ms` in every log of the trial; it is not the sync, and what the firmware waits for there is not known).
- **On the reader of the owner** (the build before this one): a book opened with Wi-Fi asleep, the thread had Wi-Fi on in 3.1 s, the place kept was sent and the place asked, the program answering meanwhile. After Wi-Fi was turned off by hand in the panel of the firmware, `NetConnectSilent` returned at once without a network, three times: the answer of the firmware and the state of the network before it go to the log now. The event 94 of the firmware (its panel is about to open) comes in a thread of the firmware, not in that of the program: left out, as the program does nothing with it.
- **Not checked on a reader**: whether the firmware lets the device sleep while the thread is in it.

### The reading position of an HTML book is not found again (alpha1)

- **A pointer through an element with a dot in its name is read back** (`ParseXPathStep`, `isXPathPointDot`, `crengine/src/lvtinydom.cpp`). A tester (the one of the Lib.ru pages): an HTML book opened for the first time, some pages read, the book left, and opened again it is at its beginning; two logs given. The logs: in the first run twelve pages are turned and the history is written at the exit; in the second run the book comes from its cache file and the engine says `LVDocView::restorePosition() - last position not found`, with no line about a size that differs, so either the history had no record of the file or the pointer of the record was not read back. The position sync is not in these logs at all. What was found on the PC bench. The HTML parser takes any `<name` of letters, digits, `-`, `_`, `.` and `:` for a tag (`isValidIdentChar`), so a `<a.b>` or a `<www.lib.ru>` in the text of a page whose text lies in `<pre>` opens an element of that name, never closed, and the rest of the book lies inside it. `ldomXPointer::toString()` writes the name as it is, `/html/body/pre/a.b/text()[15].4999`, and `ParseXPathStep` cut a name at its first `.`, the sign that begins the point at the end of a pointer: the step was an element `a` followed by an error, `createXPointer()` gave no pointer, and the position (as well as any bookmark or citation there) was lost at the next opening, with that very line of the log. Now a `.` ends a name only when it begins the point, digits up to the end of the pointer; any other `.` is a part of the name. A pointer ending in an element whose name ends in `.digits`, with no point written after it, is read as before (the name cut there); `toString()` does not write such pointers.
- **Checked on the PC bench** with a test that does what the program does: a book opened, its cache file written, a page shown, the position saved and the history file written; a second view reads the history, takes the book from the cache file and restores the position. ### Remarks of testers on alpha19 (alpha20)

- **An HTML book of Lib.ru with its only `<pre>` before the form is laid out in paragraphs** (`ldomDocumentWriterFilter::OnTagOpen`, `crengine/src/lvtinydom.cpp`). A tester: in such a book "the hyphenation works wrong, large pieces are left at the right side", and with the old engine (the releases before `0.0.7-opds`) the same files were fine; two of the files were given. The text of a Lib.ru book is plain text in `<pre>`, a line of the file every 70 characters or so, a paragraph begun by spaces. The engine knows such a book by a `<form>` in a `<div align=right>` and then builds a paragraph of the lines, but in 3.2.59 only inside a `<pre>` opened after that form. It stayed preformatted, so every line of the file was broken apart on the page and its end was left short. Now that `<pre>`, which holds nothing but the `<div>` at that moment, becomes the same `<div ParserHint="ParseAsPre">` the engine makes of a later `<pre>`, the styles of the two nodes are set again, and the text that follows is built into paragraphs. The form repeated after the text is hidden like the first one. On the PC bench (`bench-ng`, tree `a15`; `BENCH_DUMP` writes the tree of the document): "Иное небо" 1990 paragraphs and "Главный полдень" 1562, no `<pre>` left, the lines of a paragraph joined. ### Fixed (alpha12): the PDF of the tester, a textbook, as a book

The tester sent the file behind his two remarks of alpha10 that were not reproduced, "no margin at the headings" and "text set to the left edge": a preview of a textbook (42 pages of front matter: contents, prefaces, a table of cases, abbreviations). - **A heading the library guesses is not a tag of the file** (`Translator::walk`, `cr3gui/src/pb_pdftext.cpp`). With `FZ_STEXT_PARAGRAPH_BREAK` MuPDF marks a line that is underlined or set apart as `FZ_STRUCTURE_H`, with no tag name (`raw` is empty; a tag of the file has its name there). The translator took it for the structure of the file: the page numbers xiii, xv, xix at the foot of the pages and the names of courts in the table of cases came as chapters, each from a screen of its own, and a tagged item is out of reach of the rule of running titles (so the Roman page numbers of alpha10 stayed). - **A Roman number is a page number when it goes with the pages** (`romanValue`, `romanAtEdge`, `dropRunning`, `cr3gui/src/pb_pdftext.cpp`; `romanOf`, `dropRunning`, `cr3gui/src/pb_djvutext.cpp`). "xiv Preface to the Fourth Edition", "xxiii Table of Cases": a running title is found by repeating with only its digits changing; a Roman number changes its letters, and such a title may stand on a page or two only, so every one of them stayed in the text, often at the end of a paragraph. The next page has the next number: the number less the page is the same on all of them. Two lines at the top or the bottom of pages that are such a number alone are page numbers (the rule of alpha10 asked for any two), and three that begin or end with one. A count of such lines without the pages takes the headings "II", "III" of chapters and lines of the text: the first form of this change, "three lines that begin or end with a Roman number", left out of a test book the lines "...after World War II", "...King Henry IV", "...Louis XIV" at the top of its pages. - **A contents page without dots** (`endsWithPageNumber`, `TextRow::numbered`, `markEntries`, `mayBeEntry`, `Item::entry`, `findRows`, `takeText`). On such a page (`pageHanging`) a row at the margin begins an entry when the row before ends a sentence, ends with a number, or is short; and the rule "a full line that does not end a sentence goes on in the next paragraph" joins nothing there (what goes on an entry is set in, and `mendParagraphs` joins that as before). The table of cases has its 258 entries a paragraph each.

In the list of abbreviations one row ("UNCTAD United Nations Conference...") had the space too narrow and the 22 other rows of its block came glued in one paragraph. The block is text when its usual lines are a third of its rows of pieces or more. A few rows of the list with the narrow space still stand as paragraphs between the tables.

- **Levelling every page by itself: how it is done and what was measured on the PC** (`PbPageDoc::skewOfPicture`, `skewOf`, `levelView`, `pbSkewScore`, `pbSkewLines`, `pbContentOfPictureTurned`, `cr3gui/src/pb_pagedoc.cpp`, `pb_pagedoc.h`; `pagebench level`, `dev/pdfdjvu/pagebench.cpp`; `PB_SAMPLE_ANGLES`, `PB_SAMPLE_SHIFTS`, `dev/pdfdjvu/make_samples.py`). The owner asked whether the way of KOReader can find the place of every page of a crooked scan, and what it costs.
  - **KOReader** (the sources of `koreader/libk2pdfopt` read: `lib/koptimize.c`, `lib/koptcrop.c`, `willuslib/bmp.c`). The tilt is found by `bmp_autostraighten`: for every angle of the range in steps of 0.05 degrees (201 angles for five degrees to both sides) the dark pixels of the whole drawn page are summed along lines of that tilt, up to 400 points a line, and the angle at which the sums differ most is taken; then the whole picture is turned. The margins are found by the dark pixels of the rows and the columns. The code is under AGPL-3, as this line already is with MuPDF, and this part needs neither Tesseract nor Leptonica; but it is called through the settings and the page structures of the library, so `k2pdfoptlib` and `willuslib` would come nearly whole, and as it is it would take seconds a page on a reader. Not linked in.
  - **The same way in this code.** One small picture of the page, 512 pixels wide, serves the tilt and the content. The tilt: the content of the page alone, a first pass of half a degree a step on every second pixel, a second pass of a tenth of a degree around the best, the top of a parabola between the steps; the score is the mean square of the difference between the sums of neighbouring lines. Taken only when the best angle stands eight times above the usual one, does not lie at the limit of the range, and the page has six lines or more; a tilt under 0.15 degrees is none. A page of a PDF that has text is not looked at: it is drawn level by the program that made the file. The content of the levelled page is found on the same picture, its pixels counted into the columns and the rows of the turned one, so the picture is not drawn a second time and the corners of the turned page bring in nothing. The frame is drawn at any angle already, so the turn itself costs what a frame set by hand at an angle costs.
  - **Numbers of the PC bench**, the tilt looked for within 15 degrees. Photographed pages of 12 megapixels made by `make_samples.py` with known angles, each as a PDF of JPEG frames, a DjVu of photo pages and a black and white DjVu: 4 pages turned by 0.6 to 2.2 degrees, 6 by 3 to 14 degrees, 6 turned by 1 to 13 degrees and standing off the middle of the frame to every side. All 48 found; the error is 0.03 degrees or less on 45 of them and 0.08 at the most (a black and white page turned by 7 degrees). The levelled frames were looked at: the text lies level and fills the frame. Time: 5.3 to 6.0 ms a page, 9.5 ms the slowest, of which the arithmetic of the tilt is 1.0 to 1.2 ms; the page itself takes 30 to 200 ms to read and draw there. The files of the owner: his DjVu of 789 pages scanned at 600 dpi has tilts of 0.2 to 0.66 degrees that change from page to page (found on 19 pages of 40, the others level, empty or pictures; 3.5 to 4.0 ms a page); his scanned PDF 0.45 degrees on both pages. No turn on the 18 upright pages of the diagnostic files with tables, pictures and columns, none on 30 slides but one of 0.15 degrees, none on the text PDF. On a reader, which is 30 to 60 times slower, that is 0.15 to 0.35 s a page, once a page, in the pause the next page is prepared in.
  - **What a page costs with the levelling and without it** (`pagebench turns without|with`, a frame of 1404 x 1872, nothing prepared ahead, the mean of three runs, ms a page on the PC): photographed pages of 12 megapixels as a PDF 66.3 and 68.6 (3 % more), as DjVu photo pages 169.5 and 175.0 (3 %), black and white 32.9 and 39.4 (20 %); the DjVu of the owner scanned at 600 dpi 24.6 and 29.0 on forty pages with tilts (18 %), 30.9 and 35.0 on forty others (13 %); his scanned PDF 12.6 and 17.6 (40 %); his slides 26.4 and 28.5 (8 %); his text PDF 4.1 and 4.4 (its pages are not looked at). So 2 to 7 ms a page more, most of it the small picture drawn for the levelling; a frame drawn turned takes about 1 ms more than an upright one. "With" has the content box of the upright page in it as well, which a turned page does not need: the numbers are an upper bound. On a reader, 30 to 60 times slower, that is 0.1 to 0.4 s more for a page that takes 0.4 to 10 s to prepare; it is prepared in the pause, and a page prepared ahead is shown in 5 ms either way. **Two cuts of this bench were wrong before it was right:** `levelView` alone decoded a photographed page small for its picture and the frame decoded it again, at another cost than the usual way (the frames are laid out first now, which tells the document the size to decode at); and two documents of one run shared the pages DjVuLibre keeps decoded for the whole program, so the second way measured got them for nothing (one way a run now). Both showed the levelling as the faster way.
  - **Wrong on the way, found by looking at the frames:** the content of a turned page was first looked for on a second picture of the page drawn turned, whose white corners hid the dark table from the search for the paper, and the frame took in the whole table; a title page and a slide with a picture got tilts that were not there until the lines were counted; the lines were first counted by empty rows between them, and a small picture has none.

### Added (alpha3): a DjVu with a text layer is read as a book

- **The recognised text of a DjVu is written as an FB2 book, which the program opens as any book** (`pbDjvuToBook`, `pbDjvuKind`, `cr3gui/src/pb_djvutext.cpp`, `pb_djvutext.h`; kept, made and switched by the same glue as the book of a PDF, `cr3gui/src/pb_pdfbook.cpp`). The text layer is what a recognition program read from the scans: lines with their places on the page, in some files gathered into paragraphs. Reading it does not decode the pages.
  - **Paragraphs**: the ones of the file when they look like paragraphs (between 1.4 and 14 lines each on the average); else put together from the lines. A line ends its paragraph when the first word of the next one would have fitted after it (true for a ragged right edge as for an even one; the right edge is that of the few lines around, since a page has blocks set in and columns), when the next line begins further right (the first line of a paragraph; not the second line of a paragraph whose first line hangs out), after a gap, at a change of size, before a bullet or a number of a list. Pieces of one row that the file gives as lines of their own (the words of a justified line) make one line. A word cut at the end of a line is put together.
  - **Also**: headings (lines a third taller than the text; a short line set in the middle); verse and listings (`<poem>`: three lines and more where the first word of a line would mostly have fitted on the line before); lines of a contents page (a title, a row of dots, a number: a paragraph each, the dots as one mark); rows whose pieces stand at the same places as those of the row beside (`<table>`); the contents of the file as the sections of the book, the heading with the words of an entry becoming the title of its section; without contents the headings found begin the sections. Running titles and page numbers are left out (the top or the bottom line of three pages and more with only its numbers changing, of the size of the text; or of half of the pages whatever its size). A paragraph cut by the end of a page is joined. The first page is the cover; its text is left out when it has under 400 characters. `pgN` ids as in the book of a PDF.
  - **Pictures**. A page is decoded only when its text block has a place of three line steps and more with no line of the text layer in it, or no text at all. Such a place is looked at on a small picture of the page, and what is drawn there is cut out as a JPEG. The owner looked at the first cut ("many footers and headers ... a lot of rubbish; take them by size and shape, at certain distances from the top and the bottom"): **a picture is taken only inside the text block of the page** (between the usual top and bottom of the text of the file, so not from the margins of the running titles), **at least 12 mm wide and 5 mm high** and taller than 1.2 lines, not a strip (one side over twelve times the other), with at least 1/150 of it drawn; lines from the top of the place to its bottom and across it (frames, rules) are not counted; the place is taken a third of a line inside the lines around it (the boxes of a text layer are not exact and brought pieces of letters); and **a picture that stands at the same place of three pages and more and looks the same there is left out** (compared 8 by 8). On the owner's file of 84 slides: 82 cuts, mostly frames and the ornament at the bottom of every slide, became 20, all of them drawings and screenshots.
  - **A formula set on a line of its own** comes out of the recognition as a row of signs. A short paragraph with a mathematical sign and at most one word of four letters, with the short pieces right above and under it (the parts of a fraction), is cut out of the page as a picture; its text is dropped once the picture is made.
  - **A formula inside a line** that has a root, a sum, a product or an integral sign: from the first word with such a sign to the last one, with the words of a formula around them, the part of the page those words stand on is cut out and stands in the line as a picture (the text keeps a mark in its place; the words are written instead when the picture cannot be made).
  - **A picture beside the text**, which the lines flow around. The text block is cut into bands a line step high; a run of bands with text in three of them and more and 25 mm free at one side over 20 mm of height is looked at from the text to the edge of the page. A picture there is 15 mm each way at least, drawn over a fiftieth of it, and stands clear of the text: what reaches the edge on the side of the text goes on under the text (a table, a frame) and is not cut in two.
  - **A drawing with words in it** (a scheme with labels, a table of marks). Its words are lines of the text layer, so it came out as strips cut between them. A piece that reaches the edge of its place is grown over the lines there, row by row while something is drawn, to the first white band; pieces that meet are one picture; the lines of text inside it leave the text. The lines that run down the whole of the rows at hand (the frame of a slide) are not counted, a line across is passed over, and the growth does not leave the text block by more than a tenth of it. A picture that finds no white band within twelve lines is left as it was.
  - **Tables by the words of a line**: words set apart by 1.3 heights of the line are pieces of a row as the pieces given as lines of their own are. In a file without paragraphs a table is three rows and more, or rows of three cells and more.
  - An ornament is now kept where it stands first and left out after that: a drawing a file really repeats is not lost.
- **Which files**: `pbDjvuKind` looks at ten pages spread over the file; a file with a hundred characters of text on half of them or more is read as a book. A DjVu without a text layer stays in the picture mode. The setting *DjVu view settings → A file with text* (as a book / pages as they are), the item *Read as a book* of the picture mode and *Settings → Pages of the DjVu as they are* work as for a PDF.
- PC tool `dev/pdfdjvu/djvu2book.cpp` (`build_djvu2book.sh`): writes the book and prints what it made and the times by parts; `--kind`; `PB_DJVUTEXT_DUMP=1` prints the items. Checked on the diagnostic file (paragraphs of the file) and two files of the owner (lines only: a report of 11 pages with footnotes, quotations and a contents page; 84 slides): the books are well-formed.

  - The page that will be wanted next is being decoded in a thread of the library while the one before it is looked at: waiting for decoded pages 372 → 130 ms.

- `pbBlitGray` draws a gray picture through any affine map; a frame that is not turned mixes two rows of the source once into a row and then two neighbours of it (3.7 → 3.0 ms); a step over two pixels of the source takes the mean of up to 4 × 4 of them (the small picture of the content box). `pbRotateGray` is a call of it.
- A text page of a PDF is as it was (0.5–1.2 ms).

### Fixed (alpha2): the pages of the picture mode were blank

- **Every page of a PDF or DjVu was white on the reader** (`toCanvasDepth`, `PbPageWindow::drawShot`, `PbPageWindow::draw`, `cr3gui/src/pb_pageview.cpp`). The first run of alpha1 on InkPad 4: the files opened, the pages were turned, the log told a page drawn in 97 ms (PDF) and up to 2 s (DjVu, 600 dpi), and the screen stayed white. A page is rendered in 8 bits of gray; the canvas of the screen holds 4 (16 grays, one byte per pixel with the low four bits clear), and `LVGrayDrawBuf::DrawTo` copies nothing between buffers of two different depths: it returns. The window was not run on the PC, where the bench draws into its own buffer. The picture is now brought to the depth of the canvas when it is rendered (the low bits cleared four pixels at a step, so mostly in the frame prepared ahead) and handed to `DrawTo` in that depth; a canvas of 2 bits gets it packed.
- **The log tells where the time of a frame goes**: the page read and decoded, the content box, the rest is the frame (`PbPageDoc::readMs`, `boxMs`). The first run gave one number, 2 s for a page of a 600 dpi DjVu.

### Speed (alpha4)

Where the time of a layout goes was taken with a sampling profiler written into the PC bench (`BENCH_SAMPLE` of `bench2.cpp`, `sample_report.py`: the call stack every millisecond of processor time, the time by function and by source line). gprof, used before, saw a fifth of the time: its counting of every call slowed the small functions of the document storage five times.

- **The text of a paragraph goes to the formatter in one pass** (`Utf8ToUnicodeBuffer`, `crengine/src/lvstring.cpp`; `ldomNode::getTextBuffer`, `crengine/src/lvtinydom.cpp`; `LFormattedText::AddSourceLineTake`, `crengine/include/lvtextfm.h`; `renderFinalBlock`, `crengine/src/lvrend.cpp`). Every layout takes the text of every text node of the book. It was: a count of the characters of the stored UTF-8, a string object of that size, the decoding into it, and a copy of the string into a buffer of the formatter. Now the text is decoded once, straight into the buffer the formatter keeps. A text with `text-transform` and the legacy rendering mode take the former way.
- **The ancestors of a node are read once for all the selectors** (`cssChainFind`, `LVCssSelectorRule::check`, `LVStyleSheet::apply`, `crengine/src/lvstsheet.cpp`). A style sheet has many selectors with a descendant combinator (`cite p`, `epigraph p`, `body[name="notes"] section p`...). Each of them walked the document from the node to the root, a few reads of the document storage for every step, and most of them only to find that the ancestor is not there: two thirds of the time of the style pass of an FB2. The chain of the ancestors with their element names is read once for the node; a step of a selector is then a compare of two numbers. The sibling combinators and the calls outside of a style sheet walk the document as before.
- **The list of the fonts is kept between the runs** (`SetFontListCacheFile`, `registerFontsFromList`, `registerFontsFromFiles`, `cr3gui/src/cr3main.cpp`; `LVFreeTypeFontManager::RegisterFontFaces`, `GetLastRegisteredFaces`, `crengine/src/lvfont/lvfreetypefontman.cpp`). A start opened every font file of the device with FreeType to learn its faces: 188–194 files, 81–176 ms on InkPad 4. The faces are written to `system/state/cr3/fontlist.cache` with the size and the time of every font file, and the next start registers them from there. Any difference in the set of the files, their order, sizes or times, or another version of the program: the fonts are read from their files and the list is written anew.
- **Timing of the book state of the firmware** (`CRPocketBookProStateSaver::saveState`): each call of `libbookstate` and the associated file are timed. Leaving a book took 5.0 and 5.5 s in that step in the alpha3 log (0.22–0.26 s in the alpha2 log); the log did not tell which call it was.
- PC bench, the settings of the user, the style sheets of the program: a layout after a font size change 63.2 → 53.7 ms (−15 %) for an FB2 of the user of 3.8 MB, 101.5 → 85.0 ms (−16 %) for War and Peace as FB2, 162 → 153 ms (−6 %) as EPUB; the first opening −6 % for the FB2 of the user. Not measured on a device yet. The timing script of the bench (`timeit.sh`) named a style sheet file that is not in the trees since the trees are made by `bench2.sh`: its numbers before 2026-11-17 night were taken without any style sheet.
- The books of the bench: 23 instead of 12. Added the nine books of the reader of the user (eight FB2, one EPUB) and two laboratory books made by `bench-ng/make_lab_books.py`, an FB2 and an EPUB with every element the engine lays out: tables with merged cells and a table in a table, lists of every kind, footnotes, poems, citations, pictures inline, as blocks and floating, the kinds of CSS selectors, `text-transform`, preformatted text, 72 nested blocks, right-to-left text. Every page of the 23 books in two sets of settings is the same before and after the three changes; the pages are the same with the fonts registered from the kept list.

## 0.0.6-17-66-opds (not released)

Not released on its own: these changes, made on the old engine, are a part of 0.0.7-opds.

Speed review. Test builds: `0.0.6-17-66-opds-perf0` (stage timing only), `-beta1` (timing and the changes outside of the engine) `-beta2` (the engine changes), `-beta3` (the direct copy to the screen) and `-beta4` (all changes below).

Measured on InkPad 4 (743G), median of the page turns in the log: 145 ms with `-beta2` (text 43, `Stretch()` 88), 65 ms with `-beta3` (text 43, copy to the canvas 7.5).

The engine changes were checked on a PC: the engine was built for Linux before and after them, and 720 pages of three books in four configurations came out the same bit for bit, together with the position found for a tap and the text of the page.

### Changed

- **Skin caches** (`crengine/src/crskin.cpp`): 64 images and 32 skin items instead of 8. The touch menu draws 9 icons, so with 8 places every icon was read from the skin archive and decoded from PNG again each time the menu opened.
- **Reading statistics** are counted when the page has gone to the screen (`CRPocketBookScreen::flush`), not before it: the word count and the write to `cr3stats.txt` no longer delay the page. The records are the same.
- **Document buffers:** `DOC_BUFFER_SIZE` of the pro5 build is `0xA00000` (10 MB, the default of the engine) instead of 4 MB, so fewer chunks of a big book are packed and unpacked while it is read. Cache files stay compatible.
- **Glyph cache** of the pro5 build is 2 MB instead of 128 KB (`crengine/include/crsetup.h`).
- **Log:** without `crlog.ini` no log is created (it was written to `/dev/null` at the INFO level). `crengine.log.level` is read in any letter case.
- A tap looks for the text node under the finger only for the dictionary, a link and a long tap.
- The standby timer asks for the touch panel once, not on every event.
- **Page buffer copy** (`LVGrayDrawBuf::DrawTo`, `crengine/src/lvdrawbuf.cpp`): a row of a byte-per-pixel buffer is copied at once inside the clip rectangle. It was a loop with two clip tests for every pixel, run over the whole page twice at every page turn.
- **Text drawing** (`LVFreeTypeFace::DrawTextString`, `crengine/src/lvfntman.cpp`): the glyph index is looked up only when kerning is on; the second lookup of the glyph cache, whose result was thrown away, is removed.
- **Glyph cache of a font** (`LVFontLocalGlyphCache`): 256 short lists chosen by the character code instead of one list of all glyphs.
- **Page drawing** (`DrawDocument`, `crengine/src/lvrend.cpp`): a block outside of the page is skipped before its font and style are asked for. A chapter of thousands of paragraphs has them all as children of one block.
- **Cache of formatted paragraphs:** 128 instead of 32 (`crengine/src/lvtinydom.cpp`).
- **Glyph metrics** (`LVFreeTypeFace::getGlyphInfo`): the result is kept by the character code. The layout of italic text asked for the metrics of the same glyphs at every line start and end, and each request loaded and hinted the glyph again. The layout of a book with italics is about a third faster on the PC bench.
- **Page to the screen** (`CRPocketBookScreen::draw`, `cr3gui/src/cr3pocketbook.cpp`): the rows of the page are written to the screen canvas of the firmware directly, instead of the firmware call `Stretch()`. On InkPad 4 that call took 88 ms of the 145 ms a page turn is processed, twice the time of drawing the text.
  - The direct way is taken only when the canvas has one byte per pixel and the size of the page, and only after it has given the same bytes as `Stretch()` twice on this canvas. A canvas of another kind (a color screen, another firmware, an orientation the firmware turns itself) or one different byte leaves `Stretch()`, as before.
  - `cr3.pocketbook.direct.blit=0` in `cr3.ini` turns the direct way off.
  - The touch menu moves the page up on the canvas the same way, after one check against `BitmapFromScreen()` + `DrawBitmap()`, which took 90 ms of the 135 ms the menu needs to open.
  - The last pixel column of the screen is drawn now: `Draw4Bits()` left it black on a screen of an even width.
- **Background writing of the cache file** of a new book: slices of 150 ms with pauses of 450 ms instead of 500 and 1500. The share of time is the same, a tap waits a third of the former time at most.

### Fixed

- **A book downloaded from a catalog was missing on the home screen** right after exit, until the library was visited. The firmware adds a book to its list of the last opened books (`system/state/lastopen.txt`) only when it starts the reader for that book. CoolReader calls `AddLastOpen` and `FlushLastOpen` now when it opens a downloaded book itself (looked up at run time).
- `LVFreeTypeFace::measureText` wrote the flags of all characters after the fit point to one cell of the output array. No caller reads those cells today, so nothing changes on the screen.

### Added

- **Stage timing in the log** (`perf:` lines of the INFO level, written only with `crlog.ini`):
  - one line per event that reached the screen: page draw, statistics, battery, buffer compare, copy to the screen, screen update call;
  - the stages of the start: fonts, skin, settings, history, book;
  - book parsing and layout, every slice of the background cache writing;
  - the touch menu.

  - When no dictionary has the word, the list of the nearest words comes from the dictionary whose nearest word shares the longest beginning with it.
- **Reading statistics** (`cr3gui/src/pb_stats.cpp`, `pb_statsview.cpp`), opened from the new chart icon of the touch quick menu (pb740fw5, pb631fw5, pb626fw5) or from *Settings → Reading statistics*:
  - every page shown for 5 seconds or longer is written to `system/state/cr3/cr3stats.txt`, at most 2 minutes per page; standby and exit stop the clock;
  - book report: reading started and finished, reading days, pages read of the total, total time, estimated time to finish, average time per page and per day, time of each reading day;
  - period reports for today, this week, this month, this year, all time or dates typed in: pages and words with averages, books read and read to the end, total time, time by days and by books;
  - all books with their reading time.
- **Keyboard of CoolReader for the dictionary search** (`cr3gui/src/pb_keyboard.cpp`), used instead of the firmware keyboard on touch devices:
  - English, Russian and Ukrainian layouts (the layout key switches them, the last one is remembered); the keys are placed as on the firmware keyboard (`EN.kbd`, `RU.kbd`, `UA.kbd`): 5 rows of 11 keys, the digits in the first row, the signs and capital letters on the Shift page;
  - Shift for one character, space, backspace, cancel and OK; the word of the dictionary window is selected in the field and the first letter replaces it;
  - it follows the night mode of CoolReader; the firmware keyboard keeps its colors;
  - devices without a touch screen keep the firmware keyboard, whatever the setting says.
- **Dictionary settings** (*Settings → Dictionary settings*):
  - *Dictionary window*: away from the selected word, at the top or at the bottom (moved here from the display settings);
  - *Word search*: *With nearest words* (as before: a word that is not in the dictionary opens the list of the nearest words) or *Exact* (only the article of the word itself, in any letter case or through a StarDict word form; otherwise "word not found");
  - *Search keyboard*: the keyboard of CoolReader with the night mode or the firmware keyboard; shown on touch devices only.
- **Several OPDS catalogs** (*Settings → OPDS catalogs*):
  - Flibusta is always the first catalog: it cannot be renamed or deleted, its address can be changed (a mirror);
  - other catalogs are added with an address and a name, then renamed, readdressed or deleted on their card;
  - one catalog can be the default one: the catalog icon of the touch menu opens it, without a default catalog the icon shows the list;
  - *Other catalog…* in the catalog menu switches catalogs;
  - the catalogs are kept by number, `cr3.pocketbook.opds.catalog.<N>.name` / `.url` (0 is Flibusta) and `cr3.pocketbook.opds.default`; a catalog address set in an earlier version becomes a catalog of the list, or the Flibusta address when it is a Flibusta mirror.
- **Auto rotation by the G-sensor** for *Control settings → Rotate → 360°*. Firmware 6.11 sends no orientation events to CoolReader, so it asks for the orientation while the book is on the screen (twice a second on firmware 6.x, once a second from the raw sensor values): when the reader stands on a side edge (lying on one's side), the page turns by 90 degrees. Only CoolReader is rotated, the system orientation stays as it is.
  - Firmware 6.x: the orientation of the firmware (`GetGSensorOrientation`, looked up in `libinkview` at start; `ReadGSensor` returns -1 there). If the firmware sensor is off, CoolReader turns it on while it runs and off again at exit.
  - Earlier firmware: the raw sensor values (`ReadGSensor`).
  - Polling stops when the firmware sends orientation events itself.
- **Page numbers in the touch TOC:** *Display settings → Page numbers in TOC*. The classic *Chapter ……… 98* look, without separator lines between the rows.
- **Article pages in the dictionary window:**
  - a vertical swipe turns the page of the article or of the word list;
  - the title shows the page, for example *1/4*, when the article is longer than the window.
- **Ukrainian translation** of the interface and of the manual.
- **More FW5 touch skins** in the package: pb618fw5 (758×1024), pb700fw5 (1264×1680) and pbXfw5 (1404×1872, smaller fonts than pb740fw5). The pb626fw5 and pb631fw5 skins are replaced with their newer versions (pb631fw5 is now drawn for 1072×1448, with the nine-button cite toolbar). All of them have what pb740fw5 got in this version:
  - the catalog and statistics icons of the touch menu, drawn in the style of the skin (gray circle, plain, or black circle or square when pressed);
  - the `touch-toc-dots` menu for the TOC with page numbers and the plain lists;
  - dictionary window and word list titles tall enough for the larger toolbar buttons.
- **Default skin by screen size** when none is chosen: pb740fw5 from 1872 px, pb700fw5 from 1680 px, pb631fw5 above 1024 px, pb626fw5 above 800 px. pb618fw5 also uses the touch quick menu and the touch TOC.

### Changed

- **Old TOC mode** (*Use new TOC menu* off) is a plain touch list of chapters with indents and page numbers, which works with every skin. The firmware contents window used before crashes on firmware 6.11.
- **Rotate** is *360°* by default for new settings.
- **OPDS book description:** no separator lines between its rows.
- **Statistics screens:** the labels and the values are drawn in one font on one baseline (labels in gray); the report has no separator lines between rows, sections start with a line; the chart icon has the gray circle of the other icons of the row (pb740fw5, pb631fw5).
- **Navigation of the catalog and statistics screens:** the arrow at the caption and the Back key go one level up (from the catalog start page and the statistics menu, back to the book); the first row of every screen names where it goes back to; *Close catalog* is now *Back to the book*.
- **Dictionary window:**
  - its place is a setting, *Dictionary settings → Dictionary window*: away from the selected word (as before), at the top or at the bottom;
  - a vertical swipe no longer selects a word under the finger, so the window does not jump to the other half of the screen;
  - it follows the night mode (inverted display);
  - page keys and a horizontal swipe over the text turn the book page, the dictionary stays open;
  - the toolbar buttons are 1.5 times the image size, with taller titles in the FW5 touch skins;
  - a pressed button is drawn inverted until the finger is lifted, and the key cursor is not shown after a touch;
  - the word list shows no HTML tags or character entities;
  - `<br>` and `<font color>` in `.dic` articles are kept as line breaks and colors;
  - Lingvo DSL stress marks left in converted `.dic` articles (`прибл[']и[/']зно`, also with the apostrophe written as `&#x27;`) are removed from the article and the word list.
- **OPDS catalog:** its full-screen touch lists moved to `cr3gui/src/opds/opdsmenu.cpp` to be shared with the statistics screens and the contents. The unused `readingStats` stub is removed.
- **Package:** no `system/config` folder, so file associations on the device are kept.
- **Build:** `CMAKE_POLICY_DEFAULT_CMP0065=NEW` in `make.sh`. With CMake 3.4 and later the old policy linked the binary with `-rdynamic`; its exported symbols replaced those of `libinkview`, and the reader crashed at start. `gettext` is listed in the build dependencies.

### Fixed

- The old TOC mode crashed the reader on firmware 6.11.
- A dictionary chosen in the list of dictionaries was not applied when more than one dictionary was installed.
- Word list rows kept pointers into the firmware dictionary memory; they now keep copies.

### Known limits

- Firmware 6.11 keeps its own dictionaries in `/mnt/secure`, out of reach of other applications, and no longer has `OpenDictionaryView`. CoolReader reads `.dic` files from `system/dictionaries`, StarDict and XDXF dictionaries.
- The firmware on-screen keyboard (search in the book, OPDS, devices without a touch screen) keeps its colors in the night mode of CoolReader.
- XDXF dictionaries are read only as plain `.xdxf` files, not compressed.
